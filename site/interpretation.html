Sleep-staging generalization gap

Interpretation

What the results suggest and don't suggest, why the preliminary finding reversed, related work, and limitations.

What the findings suggest

At full scale, the feature model's cross-cohort macro-F1 (0.627) falls well inside its within-cassette 95% confidence interval (0.626–0.630) — the two overlap, so there is no statistically detectable difference between them (based on overlapping bootstrap confidence intervals, not a separate hypothesis test). The resulting generalization gap is essentially zero. The CNN still loses real macro-F1 moving to the unseen cohort (gap of 0.126), concentrated in Wake, whose F1 collapses from 0.95 within-cohort to 0.20 cross-cohort.

The SHAP feature-importance rank correlation between cohorts is 0.62 (Spearman, p = 0.031) — a significant, moderately strong correlation, consistent with the feature model relying on similar cues in both cohorts rather than cohort-specific shortcuts.

What this does not show: a near-zero gap on these two specific cohorts is not evidence that the feature model generalizes perfectly, or that it would hold up on a cohort that differs more (different hardware, age range, or pathology), or on a different EEG channel. It is evidence that, on this one train/test cohort pair, with this one channel, the interpretable model's macro-F1 and reasoning both transferred where the CNN's did not. It is also worth noting the within-cohort number itself may be mildly optimistic, since cross-validation groups by recording rather than by unique person — see Methods: Evaluation.

Why the preliminary finding reversed

The 10-recording run suggested the feature model generalized better than the CNN, but still with a non-trivial gap (0.266), and a feature-importance correlation that was not statistically significant (p = 0.055). Scaling to the full cohort changed both the precision of the estimates and, for the feature model, the qualitative conclusion: its gap shrank to near zero and the correlation became significant. This is the expected effect of sample size on a comparison like this one, and it is the reason the smaller run is now explicitly labeled preliminary rather than removed — the change itself is part of the honest record. See the preliminary table on the Results page.

  • AISleep (Patterns, Sept 2025) — interpretable single-channel sleep staging. PMC12745993

Limitations

  • Two specific PhysioNet cohorts, not a broad multi-site benchmark — the full cassette/telemetry cohorts are used, but still only this one dataset family.
  • Within-cohort cross-validation groups by recording, not by unique person, so a person's two nights can be split across folds — the within-cohort numbers may be mildly optimistic as a result. The cross-cohort numbers (the project's main comparison) are unaffected.
  • Single EEG channel only; multi-channel information is not used.
  • The two cohorts differ in more than one way at once (age, health status, recording era, hardware) — the study cannot fully isolate which factor drives the CNN's gap.
  • This is an educational research replication, not a validated clinical or diagnostic tool, and makes no claim to outperform published sleep-staging systems or to generalize beyond the cohorts tested here.
