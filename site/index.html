Where Does Sleep-Staging Generalization Break?

A controlled cross-cohort study comparing a deep CNN against an interpretable feature-based model on public EEG sleep data — not a claim of state-of-the-art accuracy, but a decomposition of where and why generalization fails.

Public data only (PhysioNet Sleep-EDF) Fully reproducible Educational research replication

Research question

When an EEG sleep-stage classifier trained on one cohort is applied to a different cohort (different age distribution, health status, or recording hardware), which specific signal features drive the performance drop — and can a simple, interpretable feature-based model be more robust to this shift than a deep model, even if less accurate in-domain?

Methods

Data: Sleep-EDF Database Expanded (PhysioNet, ODC-By license). Two cohorts: sleep-cassette (healthy subjects, 1987–91) and sleep-telemetry (subjects with mild sleep difficulty, 1994), single EEG channel (Fpz-Cz) for comparability.

Preprocessing: 30-second epochs, AASM 5-class staging (W / N1 / N2 / N3 / REM).

Models: a small 1D-CNN over raw epochs, and a LightGBM classifier over hand-engineered interpretable features (relative band power, spectral edge frequency, spectral entropy, Hjorth parameters).

Evaluation: subject-grouped cross-validation within a cohort (in-domain reference), vs. train-on-cassette / test-on-telemetry (cross-cohort). Macro-F1 with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals.

Interpretability: SHAP feature importances computed separately per cohort; Spearman rank correlation between the two importance rankings measures whether the model's reasoning is stable across the distribution shift.

Full code: src/. Reproduce with python src/run_pipeline.py.

Limitations

  • Small subject counts for a fast, reproducible demo run — confidence intervals are reported precisely so this is not hidden.
  • Single EEG channel only; multi-channel information is not used.
  • Two cohorts differ in more than one way at once (age, health status, recording era, hardware) — the study cannot fully isolate which factor drives the gap.
  • This is an educational research replication, not a validated clinical or diagnostic tool, and makes no claim to outperform published sleep-staging systems.
