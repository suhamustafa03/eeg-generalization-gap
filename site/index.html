Where Does Sleep-Staging Generalization Break?

A controlled cross-cohort study comparing a deep CNN against an interpretable feature-based model on public EEG sleep data — not a claim of state-of-the-art accuracy, but a decomposition of where and why generalization fails.

Public data only (PhysioNet Sleep-EDF) Fully reproducible Educational research replication

Main finding

Full cohort: 153 cassette recordings (78 people) + 44 telemetry recordings (22 people), PhysioNet Sleep-EDF Database Expanded. See Results for the complete breakdown.

Research question

When an EEG sleep-stage classifier trained on one cohort is applied to a different cohort (different age distribution, health status, or recording hardware), which specific signal features drive the performance drop — and can a simple, interpretable feature-based model be more robust to this shift than a deep model, even if less accurate in-domain?

Why this project

This engages a recent finding — "AI Generalisation Gap In Comorbid Sleep Disorder Staging" (arXiv:2603.23582, March 2026) — that deep sleep-staging models trained on healthy cohorts fail to generalize to clinical populations. Rather than repeating that result, this project decomposes it at the feature level: it trains two very different kinds of models on the same data and asks whether their reasoning (not just their macro-F1 score) survives a cohort shift, using SHAP feature-importance stability as the evidence. See Interpretation for the full related-work discussion.
